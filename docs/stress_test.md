# Stress Test Results

Stress test of the unmodified baseline using `qwen2.5:7b`.

`qwen2.5:7b` was selected for the reported stress test because it was the only candidate to meet the team's overall accuracy requirement of at least 80%, achieving 80.3%.

Raw result: `results/stress/qwen2.5-7b/custom-20261008-1311/run1.jtl`  
Timeline: `results/stress/qwen2.5-7b/custom-20261008-1311/run1.timeline.csv` / `results/stress/qwen2.5-7b/custom-20261008-1311/run1.timeline.png`

## 1. Test configuration

Stepped open-loop arrival rate:

| Stage | Offered rate | Duration |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1 | 10/min | 5 min |
| 2 | 20/min | 5 min |
| 3 | 30/min | 5 min |
| 4 | 40/min | 5 min |
| Drain | 0/min | 5 min |

The JMeter load generator and SUT ran on separate machines and communicated over a ZeroTier virtual network across separate residential internet connections. Measured response times therefore include ZeroTier and residential internet overhead.

The test was intended to identify the arrival rate at which the service could no longer keep completions in line with arrivals and latency/backlog began growing.

## 2. Execution

Before the test, the active model was verified through `GET /health` to confirm that `qwen2.5:7b` was running.

The stress test was executed from the JMeter load-generator machine using:

```powershell
.\jmeter\run_load.ps1 `
    -SutHost <SUT-IP> `
    -Kind stress `
    -Schedule "rate(10/min) random_arrivals(5 min) rate(10/min) rate(20/min) random_arrivals(5 min) rate(20/min) rate(30/min) random_arrivals(5 min) rate(30/min) rate(40/min) random_arrivals(5 min) rate(40/min) pause(5 min)" `
    -Runs 1 `
    -CooldownSec 0
```

The resulting JMeter file was analysed using:

```powershell
python scripts\analyze_stress.py "results\stress\qwen2.5-7b\custom-20261008-1311\run1.jtl"
```

## 3. Result

- **10/min:** approximately sustainable. Arrivals and completions remained close and the in-flight backlog remained relatively small.
- **20/min:** unstable. Completions consistently fell behind arrivals, maximum in-flight requests increased from 21 to 70 during the stage, and p95 latency increased from approximately 48 s to 151 s.
- **30/min:** severely overloaded. The backlog continued to grow, maximum in-flight requests reached 203, p95 latency approached 300 s, and client-side errors/timeouts began occurring.
- **40/min:** the service remained overloaded and large numbers of JMeter requests timed out before the service completed them.
- Peak observed completion throughput in a one-minute bucket was **20 tickets/min**, but this rate was not sustainable over time.

Therefore the sustainable baseline arrival-rate limit for `qwen2.5:7b` lies **between approximately 10 and 20 tickets/minute**.

The limiting behaviour was queue buildup and rapidly increasing latency. At higher offered rates, requests eventually exceeded the client's timeout even though the service continued processing them.

## 4. Reconciliation

| JMeter requests | Service requests | Count match | JMeter OK | Service OK | Client timeouts later completed by service |
|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 500 | 500 | Yes | 249 | 500 | 251 |

All **500 requests** generated by JMeter were found in the service log.

Only **249 requests completed successfully from JMeter's perspective**, while the service eventually completed all 500. The remaining **251 requests exceeded the client timeout but were subsequently completed by the service**, confirming that the failures observed under stress were caused by severe backlog and response delay rather than lost requests.

JMeter p50 was approximately **125.4 s**, while service p50 was approximately **304.5 s**. These figures should not be interpreted as normal client/network overhead because more than half of the requests timed out at the JMeter client before the service eventually completed them.

The reconciliation was produced using:

```powershell
python scripts\reconcile.py --kind stress
```
